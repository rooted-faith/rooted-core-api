# Fellowship communities with internal groups and explicit content audiences

Product-design status: accepted on 2026-11-27 after the product owner confirmed the consolidated design summary, including the subsequent navigation, map, notification, archival, and account-deletion decisions. The grill-with-docs interview is complete. This acceptance covers product behavior and design direction; formal API contracts, the remaining prototype coverage, and production implementation are separate follow-up work.

Published product specification: [ROO-122](https://linear.app/jayhsia/issue/ROO-122), synchronized from [rooted-faith/rooted_flutter#152](https://github.com/rooted-faith/rooted_flutter/issues/152), in Linear Project `rooted flutter`, with `spec` and `ready-for-agent` labels and Backlog status at publication. The specification includes the product-owner-confirmed test boundaries and the canonical HTML prototype as a Linear attachment. Repository-scoped implementation tickets have not been created by this publication step.

Rooted distinguishes a Fellowship (團契) from its internal Groups (小組), instead of treating the two terms as synonyms. An End user may join multiple Fellowships; within each Fellowship, Group Leaders and Members may belong to only one Group. The first release has four fixed roles: Owner, Admin, Group Leader, and Member. Permissions are centrally defined so they can evolve, without user-editable permission matrices or custom roles in this release. The accepted Owner/Admin plus Group Leader combination remains supported. Each Group has a Group Leader.

Published content may target the whole Fellowship, one Group, or several selected Groups. Owners and Admins can read all published fellowship content, while private journals, personal prayers, and unshared lesson notes remain private. Explicit audiences support cross-group fellowship without forcing every contribution into either a single-group space or a fellowship-wide feed.

Fellowship and Group membership limits are controlled by system settings, initially 50 and 15 people respectively. Fellowships remain invitation-based, with links and QR codes shareable by Owners and Admins. Each invitation can require approval or allow direct admission, with a choice of 7-day, 30-day, or permanent validity, defaulting to 7 days. An active invitation permits multiple people to join, subject to the Fellowship membership limit. Revocation invalidates the link and its QR code together. Invitations admit to the Fellowship only; administrators assign Groups afterward, avoiding separate group-specific invitation management.

A Fellowship's public/private classification controls only eligibility for future search, which is deferred. Whether an individual content item is publicly readable is a separate choice; public/private Fellowship classification does not grant or revoke that access. Posts cover fellowship life and general conversation, while Shares cover devotional insights and faith responses. Only posts, announcements, and gatherings may be public; prayer requests and devotional Shares remain internal. Public publication is initially limited to Owners and Admins and requires a Fellowship-wide audience, rather than directly exposing Group-restricted content. Public reading excludes comments, membership lists, attendance lists, and prayer-participant identities.

Owner and Admin roles do not require Group membership. If an Owner or Admin joins a Group, that person also holds the Group Leader role for that Group. Administrative access across Groups is independent of Group membership.

Content stays in its original audience when its author changes Groups. Moving Groups grants access to the new Group's history and removes access to the former Group's restricted content, except for an own-content viewing/withdrawal path. Leaving a Fellowship removes all internal-content access, including access to the former member's own publications; no own-content exception survives departure. This avoids a separate former-member access model. Public-content access is independent of membership.

Initial publishing permissions distinguish content types: Owners and Admins may publish posts, announcements, gatherings, prayer requests, and Shares to the Fellowship, one Group, or multiple Groups. Group Leaders may publish announcements and gatherings to their own Group, and posts, prayer requests, and Shares to their own Group or the whole Fellowship. Members may publish posts, prayer requests, and Shares to their own Group or the whole Fellowship, but do not create announcements or gatherings. These are initial permissions rather than a fixed, irreversible role hierarchy.

Authors may edit or withdraw their own publications. Owners and Admins may hide inappropriate fellowship content; Group Leaders may hide it within their own Group. Moderators do not rewrite another author's text. Announcement pinning follows the same Fellowship/Group management boundaries. Expanding the audience of an existing publication requires a new publication, so existing private comments are not exposed to a larger audience.

Reports enter a platform Admin moderation queue rather than a fellowship-App review queue. The reported person does not receive the reporter's identity. Authorized fellowship managers may hide content locally, but a single report does not automatically delete content or suspend its author. Platform review is handled through the admin console.

Voluntary departure permits later rejoining through a valid invitation and its approval policy. Administrative removal blocks invitation-based reentry until an Owner or Admin lifts the restriction. Rejoining starts as an unassigned Member rather than restoring an old role or Group automatically.

Account deletion distinguishes personal publications from fellowship administration data. The deleted person's posts, devotional Shares, prayer requests, and comments are deleted. Private journals, personal prayers, and unshared lesson notes remain private and are deleted rather than transferred to a Fellowship. Announcements and gatherings remain with the Fellowship, with the author's account association and personal attribution removed and the author displayed as "此帳號已刪除". This revises the older blanket "delete all cloud data" promise: the end-user deletion description and API specification must disclose the retained fellowship administration records.

Before account deletion, an Owner must transfer ownership or explicitly dissolve each Fellowship they own. Other role and membership associations are removed. The last Group Leader is guided toward handover, but an incomplete handover does not prevent account deletion: retain the Group with a visible "待指派小組長" state and notify Owners/Admins to appoint a replacement. Do not automatically dissolve or archive that Group. This is an explicit exception to the ordinary departure/transfer/demotion succession rule, so deleting one's account does not depend on another person completing Group administration.

Group reorganization creates new Groups rather than repurposing old Group identities. Authorized users subsequently archive the old Groups. Historical content remains attached to the original Groups and is not copied into the replacement audiences; archived Groups accept no new publications. This preserves the original disclosure boundary through reorganization.

Role administration is explicit: only the Owner transfers ownership or appoints/removes Admins. Owners and Admins create/archive Groups, assign/transfer Group membership, appoint/remove Group Leaders, approve admission, remove members, and lift reentry restrictions. Group Leaders manage care and eligible content within their own Group. Ordinary departure, Group transfer, and role demotion must not leave an operating Fellowship without its sole Owner or an operating Group without its last Group Leader; arrange succession or authorized dissolution/archival first. Account deletion follows the explicit exception described above.

A Group Leader may hide a publication only when it targets that single Group. Multi-Group and Fellowship-wide publications can be reported by Group Leaders but require Owner/Admin authority for whole-publication hiding. Per-Group hiding of a shared publication is excluded initially.

Archived Groups allow no new publications, comments, or prayer actions. Owners and Admins may read historical content. Authors who remain Fellowship members may view and withdraw their own publications but not edit them. Former Group members do not retain access to other restricted publications after transfer. Replacement Groups start with separate content spaces.

The first design includes prayer and care, devotional sharing, and fellowship-life functions: announcements, regular gatherings, one-time gatherings or activities, membership administration, and content comments. Regular gatherings have an established recurring time and do not request attendance responses; attendance responses apply to one-time gatherings or activities. Weekly recurrence is the initial prototype scope. After reviewing the separate Q26 prototype, the product owner accepted single-occurrence rescheduling/cancellation and changes to the recurring arrangement from a specified effective date, with a before/after preview. These changes do not introduce attendance responses for regular gatherings. Existing explicit occurrence exceptions survive subsequent recurring-rule changes and are listed in the preview; managers may explicitly restore an occurrence rather than having an exception silently overwritten. Additional recurrence patterns are outside the initial weekly scope. A standalone chat room is deferred. The Fellowship tab opens the End user's explicitly selected Primary Fellowship homepage on each entry; switching Fellowships for browsing does not change that preference. The homepage summarizes content with dedicated lists and publishing flows.

Member care (成員關懷) replaces the unclear Walk together (同行) feature name. Members can submit or cancel their own care requests. Owners and Admins can view and manage care across the Fellowship; Group Leaders are limited to their own Group. Other members cannot view these care records. Member-originated requests and manager-originated tracking remain separate: acknowledging a request does not cancel it, and completing tracking does not erase an outstanding request. Manager tracking uses pending, following up, and completed states with update actor and time. No free-text pastoral notes or automatic App-activity-based care labels are included initially. The weekly invitation feature is deferred.

Notifications cover membership applications for Owners/Admins; application outcomes, Group assignments, and role changes for the affected person; new announcements, one-time events, and gathering schedule changes/cancellations for the relevant audience; optional comments/prayer notifications for the content author; and care requests for the person's Group Leaders, falling back to Owners/Admins for unassigned members. Ordinary posts and Shares do not each trigger push notifications. Care push copy is generic and does not expose the request's sensitive content. Notification category preferences are set independently by each End user for each Fellowship, allowing different notification choices across their Fellowships. All listed categories default to enabled, with comment and prayer notifications independently switchable, and management-only categories shown only to eligible roles. A per-Fellowship pause-all-push option does not prevent reading content in the App. A separate notification inbox is excluded from the first release.

The reviewed artifact is one interactive HTML prototype derived from `rooted_flutter/temp/app_main_layout.html`, using in-memory sample data. On 2026-11-27, the product owner confirmed the current pages and principal interaction flows after reviewing navigation, homepage actions, and gathering types. This confirms the prototype design direction; it does not establish completed backend contracts, Flutter implementation, native-device acceptance, or live API readiness.

After the separate Q26 gathering-change exploration was accepted, the product owner requested a single prototype. Gathering-change interactions are consolidated into `fellowship.prototype.html` alongside the separate Member/Group pages and contextual Popup Menus; the separate gathering-change preview is retired. The consolidated HTML remains a partial interaction artifact, not evidence that every documented feature has been implemented.

The homepage uses an ellipsis popup menu for publishing, role-appropriate fellowship options, and invitation access for Owners and Admins. Secondary pages center the Fellowship name in the top bar, place Back on the left, and reserve the right side for that page's actions. Back returns to the preceding page and its reading position. Form sheets use the same arrangement, with contextual submit actions such as Publish or Save.

Across all fellowship pages and form presentations, two or more available top-right actions collapse into one ellipsis Popup Menu. A single available action remains directly accessible; no action produces no button. Count actions after applying the current user's permissions. The homepage retains its explicitly selected Popup Menu presentation.

Members and Groups have separate navigation entries and list pages, rather than a combined members-and-groups screen. This gives the Group list its own filtering surface independently of member administration. The membership relationship and authorization boundaries remain shared domain rules, not separate copies of membership data.

Gatherings support in-person, online, and hybrid formats. Hybrid gatherings offer both a physical location and an online meeting link. Physical locations support an in-App map preview and external navigation: previews use Apple Maps/MapKit on iOS and Google Maps on Android. Selecting navigation opens a map-App chooser rather than forcing the preview provider; only supported, available Apps should be offered, with a browser fallback if needed. In-App place search, draggable location selection, and in-App navigation are excluded from this scope. Both preview implementations should represent the same gathering destination. Operational setup and address-to-coordinate resolution remain technical details to resolve.

This records the decisions confirmed during redesign, not a completed API contract. The earlier model of independent small fellowships and the existing Flutter screens are reference material rather than the implementation contract for this design.
